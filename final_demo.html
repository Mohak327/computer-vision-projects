Part B — MNIST Denoising + Flow Matching
MNIST denoising and flow matching

From noisy digits to guided generation.

This page turns the notebook output into a compact story: a single-step denoiser learns to recover clean digits, flow matching learns to transform noise into samples over time, and class conditioning makes the digits much more coherent. The gallery below includes every saved figure from the notebook outputs folder.

14 saved figures 2 model families 3 sampling settings 10 training epochs

What to notice

Fast loss collapse All three training curves drop sharply in the first few hundred iterations.
OOD noise degrades gracefully The denoiser works best around the training sigma and gets weaker as noise moves out of range.
Conditioning matters Class-conditioned flow matching produces much sharper, class-faithful digits than the unconditional version.

Part 1: Single-step Denoising UNet

The denoiser is trained on MNIST with Gaussian noise at sigma = 0.5. The figures below show how the model behaves as noise increases, how training improves reconstruction, and where the one-step assumption starts to break.

Interpretation The denoising UNet behaves like a reliable one-step editor rather than a full generator. It excels at cleaning up inputs that resemble the training distribution, but it cannot invent consistent digits from arbitrary Gaussian noise.

Part 2: Flow Matching

This section switches from denoising to flow matching, where the network learns a velocity field that transports noise toward data over many small Euler steps.

Interpretation Flow matching is doing the right kind of work here: it learns a continuous transport process instead of a one-shot reconstruction. The training curve and samples together show that the model learns directionality early, then slowly improves sample quality.

Part 3: Class-Conditioned Flow Matching

Adding class labels plus classifier-free guidance makes the sampling task far easier. The loss becomes smoother and the generated digits become much more class-faithful.

Interpretation Class conditioning changes the problem from vague generation to targeted generation. With the label as an anchor, the model no longer has to guess the digit class, so the sample quality jumps noticeably.

Part 4: Learning Rate Scheduler Comparison

The final experiment compares the class-conditioned run with and without an exponential learning-rate scheduler. The curves are close, and the sample grids remain strong in both settings.

Interpretation To compensate for removing the scheduler, we used a smaller constant learning rate (1e-3) with Adam instead of the more aggressive scheduled setup. This keeps updates stable throughout training, reduces late-epoch oscillation, and still reaches comparable loss and sample quality in this experiment.
